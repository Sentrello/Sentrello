# Newsletter

Run a mailing list properly: lists, subscribers, campaigns, and honest numbers
about what happened to each message.

## Lists and subscribers

A **list** is a group of people who agreed to hear from you. It can be public
or private, and single or double opt-in.

:::info[Double opt-in is a property of the list]
On a double opt-in list, nobody is written to until they confirm. In several of
the markets Sentrello is sold into, the difference between that and not is the
difference between marketing and an offence.
:::

Subscribers carry whatever attributes you want to keep, and can be imported
from a spreadsheet or exported in full.

**An import does not resubscribe somebody who asked to stop.** An import is the
business speaking; a subscribe form is the person speaking, and only the second
undoes an unsubscribe.

## Signup forms

The usual way anybody joins a mailing list: a form on your own website. Build
one, choose the lists a signup joins, and paste the snippet into any page —
including a site that has nothing to do with Sentrello.

A form asks for an email address, and for a name as well if you want one. Lists
set to double opt-in still send their confirmation email, so a signup here
joins on exactly the terms the list already has.

**A signup can also become a CRM contact**, per form, and it is off unless you
turn it on. Whether it is worth turning on depends on what the form asks for: an
address on its own makes a contact with no name against it, which is a record
somebody has to tidy later, while an address and a name makes one worth keeping.
The builder says which of the two you have. It needs the CRM installed; without
it the switch simply does nothing.

## Campaigns

Write a campaign, choose its lists, send a test to yourself, then schedule or
send it. Templates give you a consistent header, footer and colour.

Sending happens in batches so a large campaign does not make the rest of the
application unresponsive. A campaign can be paused and resumed, and **nobody is
written to twice** — the recipients are written down before sending starts, and
each moves from pending to sent exactly once.

Somebody on three of a campaign's lists receives one email.

## Counting

Opens and clicks are counted **only if you turn them on**, and there is a third
setting: keep the counts without recording who made them.

Both carry obligations in the markets this is sold into, which is why neither
is on by default.

## Unsubscribing

Every message carries an unsubscribe link that works from a phone, in any mail
client, with no account and no JavaScript. An unsubscribe link that sometimes
does not work is not a bug — it is a fine.

## Subscribers are not customers

They are kept separately and linked to a CRM contact where the address already
matches. Most subscribers gave you an email address and nothing else; counting
them as customers would make every count of "how many customers do we have"
wrong.
