# Dashboard

The screen you land on. It answers what needs attention today, without opening
anything.

## What is on it

- **Money**: what is owed to you, what is overdue, what came in this month.
- **Work**: deals in progress, tasks due, anything already late.
- **Whatever your modules add.**

That last one is the useful one. There is no separate reporting module here;
each module puts its own figures on this screen. Install Booking and your
upcoming appointments turn up. Install Documents and a certificate about to
expire turns up alongside them.

## Tabs, and arranging them

The panels sit on tabs: Overview, Sales, Reports and so on, plus one per module
you have installed. The default arrangement is meant to be lived with. If your
business wants something else, **Arrange** on the dashboard renames tabs, adds
and removes them, and decides what sits on each. Free and Pro arrange the same
way. Arranging is not a paid feature.

The arrangement belongs to the business rather than to whoever saved it.
Everyone sees the same tabs, so "look at the Shop tab" means one thing across
the whole company. A module you add later puts its panel on a tab of its own,
without anybody arranging anything.

## Why that matters

The most expensive thing a small business misses is a date nobody was looking
at. An insurance certificate that lapsed in March. An invoice quietly sixty
days overdue.

Those things belong on the screen everybody opens anyway, not on a report
somebody remembers to run.

## Setting up

While there is setting up left to do, the dashboard carries a checklist of it,
one section per module, drawn from what each module says its own first steps
are. Nothing on it is required. Each step ticks itself off once the thing it
describes has actually happened, however it happened.

Hide the checklist whenever you like, part-way through included. Nothing is
lost. Settings offers to bring it back for as long as steps remain, and it
resumes where things actually stand: anything done in the meantime is already
ticked. A finished checklist leaves on its own.

A module added later brings its own checklist, even years after the rest was
finished. Steps the business already satisfies show as done from the start.

## What you see

The dashboard shows only what your permissions allow. Somebody without access
to the books sees no money figures at all. Those panels are filtered by the
same rules as everything else; the browser is not hiding anything.
