# Dashboard

The screen you land on. It answers what needs attention today, without opening
anything.

## What is on it

- **Money** — what is owed to you, what is overdue, what came in this month.
- **Work** — deals in progress, tasks due, anything already late.
- **Whatever your modules add.**

That last point is the useful one. Reporting is not a separate module here:
each module contributes its own figures to this screen. Install Booking and
your upcoming appointments appear; install Documents and a certificate about to
expire appears.

## Why that matters

The single most expensive thing a small business misses is a date nobody was
looking at — an insurance certificate that lapsed, an invoice that went sixty
days overdue, a licence that expired quietly.

Those things belong on the screen everybody opens anyway, not on a report
somebody remembers to run.

## Setting up

While there is setting up left to do, the dashboard carries a checklist of it
— one section per module, drawn from what each module says its first steps
are. Nothing on it is required, and each step ticks itself off when the thing
it describes has actually happened, however it happened.

You can hide the checklist at any point, part-way through included. Nothing is
lost: Settings offers to bring it back for as long as steps remain, and it
resumes where things stand — anything done in the meantime is already ticked.
A finished checklist simply leaves on its own.

A module added later brings its own checklist, even years after the rest was
finished. Steps the business already satisfies show as done from the start.

## What you see

The dashboard shows only what your permissions allow. Somebody without access
to the books does not see the money figures — the panels are filtered by the
same rules as everything else, not hidden by the browser.
