# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume they are trying to help. Say what you mean without
making it personal.

Most of this document exists so that the rare bad day has a written answer
rather than an improvised one. If you only read the paragraph above, you will
almost certainly be fine.

## Who this covers

Everybody taking part in this project: issues, pull requests, discussions,
commit messages, code review, and any Sentrello space online or in person. It
applies to maintainers exactly as much as to first-time contributors, and it
applies to how we behave towards people who are not here to defend themselves.

## What is expected

- **Take the other person seriously.** A question that seems obvious to you was
  not obvious to whoever asked it.
- **Criticise the work, not the worker.** "This query will lock the table under
  load" is useful. "Did you even think about this?" is not, and says nothing
  the first sentence did not.
- **Accept that you might be wrong.** Including when you are the maintainer,
  and especially when the person disagreeing with you is new.
- **Let people leave.** Nobody owes this project their evenings. An unfinished
  contribution is not a debt.
- **Respect the answer "no".** A change can be well made and still not belong
  in the product. That decision is not a judgement of the person who wrote it.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment of any kind, public or private.
- Demeaning, insulting or derogatory comments — including about somebody's
  competence, and including when framed as a joke.
- Personal attacks, or bringing somebody's identity, background, employer or
  private life into a technical disagreement.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing anybody's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption: reopening a settled decision to wear people down, or
  making a space unpleasant enough that others stop taking part.

Not being intended badly is not a defence. The effect on the person on the
receiving end is what matters, and "I was only joking" is a sentence people
reach for after the damage.

## If something goes wrong

Report it to **conduct@sentrello.com**. That address reaches Sentrello LLC
directly.

Say what happened, where, and roughly when — a link is ideal, but a description
is fine. You do not need to prove anything, quote a clause of this document, or
be certain it counts. Ask if you are unsure; asking is not an accusation.

If your report is about a maintainer, say so and it will be handled by somebody
else. If that is not possible because the project is small, you will be told
that plainly rather than quietly handled by the person you complained about.

**What happens next:** you get an acknowledgement within three working days and
an outcome within fourteen, or an explanation of why it is taking longer. Your
report is kept confidential, and your name is not passed on without asking you
first. Reporting something in good faith will never count against you, even if
the outcome is that nothing happened.

## What we might do

Roughly in order, and not necessarily starting at the top:

1. **A quiet word** — usually enough, and usually the end of it.
2. **A public correction**, where the behaviour was public and left a wrong
   impression standing.
3. **A temporary ban** from the project's spaces.
4. **A permanent ban.**

Anything beyond the first step is explained to the person it applies to,
including what would have to change. Bans are not announced with a name unless
somebody's safety depends on it.

## A note on this document

It is deliberately written in plain language rather than adapted from a
template. If a situation arises that it does not obviously cover, that is a
gap in the document and not a loophole — the first section is the intent, and
the intent is what will be applied.
